# Context Driven Development
Context Driven Development (CDD) is a heavenly high level language to build products (app, business, your life). Sounds cool but what does that mean? Well it might be easier to describe it simply as a tool that hands you a nicely bundled context that humans/agents can read to deliver a product. This context contains about 3 levels of hierarchy; philosophy, behavior, and systems/decisions. The goal of CDD is to capture the entirety of context in a written language up until a boundary named “Experience”. It borrows from BDD (Behavior-driven development) and stands on the shoulders of gherkin syntax (https://cucumber.io/docs/gherkin/reference). I believe CDD can bring all roles of product development closer to raw experience through human senses. I also believe we need to define our methodology to development as its own product alongside the apps we are building to correctly have agents assist in the best way they can.

“callout” table form or bulleted list or just bunch of badges?
It is docs, design, architecture/spec, test driven plan, business logic (separate from code), user feedback, tickets, retros/learning, UI Copy, releasing strategy, product roadmap in a handful of short markdown docs. It's statically checked to fit and cap forms that then provide the correct context to test your product and let’s code follow. Some are living and some are throwaway. 

Little backstory, I've developed this while building out a django app for a good friend running a local farm. I hit every hurdle and pain point. It was easy to build but I built the wrong thing many times. It forced me to get better at interviews and to fill different roles; product owner and designer. Seems silly 20/20 hindsight that I dove into the details of how their operation worked without understanding the people that worked there (even though I worked alongside them in the past!). The who, what, when, where and why was inferred while I failed to grasp the how.

## Context
What is it? It's synonymous with one's environment or spacetime. You are experiencing some resemblance of context as we speak. 

“callout”
“It is Nov 9 2026 at 10:38am and I am drafting an article of the wiggly world defined with CDD at my desk while occasionally peering outside at the frost bitten grass”. 

It really is that simple, it's your “space” in an event of “time”. We all understand this as a core foundation of our personality. It is something we sense by seeing, touching, hearing, tasting, and smelling. Our data stream of senses come together to build a world for us we "experience" through a 6th sense.. proprioception, or our third eye. We are gathering feedback from the world and making decisions based on our philosophies and behaviour to familiar scenarios we encounter.I want to go a step deeper because I used a tool to convey that context to you… language! 

## Language
This is the root of context and every 2-3 year old gets it. Language is our tool to communicate context across time (past, present, future) in an elegant fashion. It's used to document history, journal one's thoughts and plan for the future. Some people are great at using language and writing while others are not. So language is a tool that has a certain “Quality” (zen and art of motorcycle maintenance).

## Experience
I alluded to it earlier but what is it? It's the present moment. It's all your senses coming together to build and create a context out of the wiggly undefined world that surrounds us. We separate ourselves and everything else is mixed in. Rarely do we fully experience the present moment because of our anxieties to plan and review our lives. So it really again is spacetime bundled up nicely in a context for us “humans” to work to brew some coffee or hunt some prey.
We attempt to communicate this with language. 

“callout”
We certainly only communicate to LLM (large language models) through language but that's not entirely how humans communicate. 

Humans can feel and sense in mysterious ways and that’s our “niche” for the new evolving ecosystem of AI. Humans experience and use language as an artifact to bridge spacetime. We can then Plan (future), Deliver (present) and Learn (past). We want to know where we are going, what we are doing, and what we did. Experience is something we can’t quite define and humans “feel” some certain amount of “quality” to things. This is the system boundary of CDD and where it begins… after language.

## So what is CDD exactly?
Defined by opus “How a small team of people and agents turn a customer's feedback into a plan that delivers software and learns from each cycle.”

## Why and who is this for?
Make your team's workflow something agents can run and people can read, so the work goes to plan with no surprises. The unattainable goal is a plan with no misses. Its use is overwhelming. Your business logic is now separated from your code which makes throwing away an entire app easy. The foundations and design is the same while your architecture changes. It’s everything you care about as someone who delivers a product from general philosophy to tests no matter your role (c-suite -> customer)

## When and where?
Adopt personally and see if you can find a version that fits your team. As a developer, I find myself contemplating foundations and design more now than ever and it's inspiring. Start small, perhaps your cycle is every ticket and you take a kanban approach to building up your context of the systems. Start at the foundations and do it today! It most likely already exists at your company so ask around. Build your workflow with CDD and also build your product.

## How does it work?
I recommended throwing away all the skills from the repo and starting fresh for how you work. This is an example of how I worked wearing many different hats. Start fresh and keep the forms and foundations the same. Use claude code, pi, or copilot as your harness. Choose your own adventure. It's a decision in architecture and it's a form that's easily thrown away. Establish some principles that fit you as an individual. There are so many existing in the world. Capture the ones you feel closest to as our agents can't do this. Fill the niche.

“callout”
Be warned, don't start with architecture and decisions first and build your design/foundations around it. That's going against the stream and it's costly. I've made this mistake too many times as a techy dev.
